# Energy Bites — Recommended Add-Ins

Upgrades for the Peanut Butter Chocolate Energy Bites to better support breastfeeding. All amounts are per single batch — multiply x3 for a monthly triple batch.

---

## Lactation Support

These are the classic ingredients found in most lactation bite recipes. They're traditionally used to support milk supply, though evidence is mostly anecdotal. At minimum they add real nutritional value for postpartum recovery.

**Brewer's yeast — 2 Tbsp**

- Rich in B-complex vitamins, protein, and selenium
- The #1 ingredient that turns a regular energy bite into a lactation bite
- Buy _debittered_ brewer's yeast — regular tastes terrible
- Available at Vitamin Shoppe, Amazon, or sometimes Walmart's supplement aisle
- Mild flavor, disappears into the peanut butter and chocolate

**Ground flaxseed meal — 2 Tbsp**

- Contains phytoestrogens that may support milk production
- Ground is better than whole — easier to digest and absorb
- Also adds omega-3 fatty acids (important for baby's brain development)
- Store in the fridge after opening to prevent the fats from going rancid
- Costco sells a big bag, keeps well in the fridge for months

**Hemp hearts — 2 Tbsp**

- Complete protein with all essential amino acids
- More omega-3s on top of the flax
- Soft, slightly nutty, blends right in
- Costco carries these in bulk
- Good protein boost for the overall recipe

### Lactation add-in cost per triple batch

- Brewer's yeast: ~6 Tbsp
- Ground flaxseed: ~6 Tbsp
- Hemp hearts: ~6 Tbsp

---

## Fiber Boost

Postpartum constipation is very common, and breastfeeding increases fluid demands which can compound it. The current recipe has ~1.5g fiber per ball. These changes roughly double or triple that without changing the flavor or texture.

**Double the chia seeds — from 1 Tbsp to 2 Tbsp**

- About 5g fiber per tablespoon
- They gel slightly which actually helps the balls hold together better
- No flavor change, they just disappear into the mix
- Already in the recipe, so this is the lowest-effort upgrade

**Swap 1/4 cup oats for oat bran — 1/4 cup**

- Oat bran has ~3.5g fiber per 1/4 cup vs ~1.3g for regular oats
- Mixes right in, no texture change
- Same aisle as the oats at any grocery store

**Chopped Medjool dates — 2-3 dates, finely chopped**

- Adds ~4g fiber per batch
- Natural sweetness means you can back off the honey a bit
- Traditional postpartum recovery food in many cultures
- Makes the balls slightly chewier in a good way
- Optional — changes the flavor/texture more than the others

### What to skip

- **Psyllium husk** — makes the balls gummy and weird
- **Raw wheat bran** — tastes like cardboard
- **Fiber supplement powders** — unnecessary when you can just use real food

### Fiber add-in cost per triple batch

- Chia seeds: extra 3 Tbsp (on top of existing 3 Tbsp)
- Oat bran: 3/4 cup (replacing 3/4 cup of the oats)
- Dates (optional): 6-9 Medjool dates, finely chopped

---

## Estimated Per-Ball Comparison

| Version | Calories | Protein | Fiber |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Current recipe | ~120 | ~4g | ~1.5g |
| With all add-ins | ~140 | ~5.5g | ~3.5-4g |

At 3-4 balls a day that's 12-16g fiber toward the 25-30g daily target, plus meaningfully better omega-3s, B-vitamins, and protein.
